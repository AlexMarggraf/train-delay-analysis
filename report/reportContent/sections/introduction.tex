\section{Introduction}
% Problemstellung, Analyse Ziel, Datensaetze und ER Diagramme der einzelnen Datensaetze

The goal of our project was to identify interesting datasets,
create a consistent schema that includes all data sources,
clean and integrate the data into a single database,
and analyze the data to gain meaningful insights.
Throughout this process,
we aimed not only to uncover interesting facts
about our chosen datasets
and the real-world circumstances they model,
but also to develop knowledge and skills
in finding, cleaning, and integrating
real-world, messy data
into one coherent database.\\\\
The datasets we selected consisted of train and weather data
from Switzerland and Germany, which we obtained from the internet. \cite{piebro_deutsche-bahn-data}\cite{dwd_opendata}\cite{meteoswiss_opendata}\cite{opentransportdata_switzerland_archive}
The German train data was provided by Deutsche Bahn
and consists of a collection of \texttt{.parquet} files
which list all recorded train stops
of trains operated by Deutsche Bahn in Germany.
The collection was already processed
such that there is one file per month,
which records, among other things,
where and when each train stopped
and whether it was delayed or canceled.
An ER diagram can be seen in \Cref{fig:g5_german_train_er}.\\\\

The German weather data was provided by the Deutsche Wetterdienst
and consists of a complex collection of text files
which are structured similarly to CSV files.
These files are separated by measurement type,
including temperature, precipitation, pressure, and wind,
as well as by historical data up to 2025
and recent data.
The files themselves contain the corresponding measurements,
the time at which each measurement was taken,
and the station at which it was recorded.
Additionally, the Deutsche Wetterdienst provided metadata
for the weather stations,
which offers insight into their individual locations.
An ER diagram can be seen in \Cref{fig:g5_german_weather_er}.\\\\

The Swiss train data was provided by SBB
and consists of a collection of large CSV files
which list all recorded train stops
of trains operated by SBB on a monthly basis.
This includes, among other information,
the locations at which trains stopped,
their exact arrival and departure times,
and whether the trains were canceled.
An ER diagram can be seen in \Cref{fig:g5_swiss_train_er}.\\\\

The Swiss weather data was provided by MeteoSchweiz
and consists of a collection of large CSV files
which list all measured meteorological variables,
such as temperature or precipitation,
when these measurements were taken,
and which station recorded them.
Additionally, we obtained a list of all Swiss weather stations
recorded by MeteoSchweiz,
which can be found here: \\
\url{https://data.geo.admin.ch/ch.meteoschweiz.ogd-smn/ogd-smn_meta_stations.csv}.

Our goal was to determine the delays and cancellation rates of trains in both countries.
To achieve this, we considered only data ranging from
the beginning of September 2024 to the end of August 2025.
Furthermore, we divided the data into two groups, one for each country,
and analyzed delays per year, per month, per weather condition,
and per canton or municipality.
Additionally, we calculated the percentage of train cancellations
per canton or municipality
and identified which locations experience higher traffic on average.
